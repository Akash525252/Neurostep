Deep dive

Hydrotherapy: why it matters, and why it's hard to get outside a clinic

Water supports body weight, which means a spinal cord injury or stroke patient can practise standing, stepping and balance work far earlier — and with far less fall risk — than they safely could on land. The resistance and buoyancy of water also lets therapists load muscles gently as strength returns, instead of jumping straight from bed rest to full gravity.

This is exactly the kind of equipment a home-visit therapist cannot bring with them: a hydrotherapy pool is fixed infrastructure. It's one of the clearest reasons in-clinic care outperforms home-based therapy for these conditions, and it's why NEUROSTEP built the clinic around it rather than treating it as an optional extra.
